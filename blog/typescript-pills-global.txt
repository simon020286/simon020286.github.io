d:T1b44,<p>Quante volte hai trovato metodi o oggetti direttamente definiti nell’ambito globale, pronti per essere usati, solo per scoprire che TypeScript non riconosceva la loro definizione?</p>
<pre class="shiki shiki-themes material-theme-darker material-theme-lighter" style="background-color:#212121;--shiki-dark-bg:#FAFAFA;color:#EEFFFF;--shiki-dark:#90A4AE" tabindex="0"><code><span class="line"><span style="color:#C792EA;--shiki-dark:#9C3EDA">const</span><span style="color:#EEFFFF;--shiki-dark:#90A4AE"> value </span><span style="color:#89DDFF;--shiki-dark:#39ADB5">=</span><span style="color:#EEFFFF;--shiki-dark:#90A4AE"> window</span><span style="color:#89DDFF;--shiki-dark:#39ADB5">.</span><span style="color:#82AAFF;--shiki-dark:#6182B8">glob</span><span style="color:#EEFFFF;--shiki-dark:#90A4AE">()</span></span></code></pre>
<p>In questo caso, TypeScript solleverebbe un’eccezione, affermando che l’oggetto window non dispone del metodo <code>glob()</code>.
La soluzione rapida solitamente è stata questa:</p>
<pre class="shiki shiki-themes material-theme-darker material-theme-lighter" style="background-color:#212121;--shiki-dark-bg:#FAFAFA;color:#EEFFFF;--shiki-dark:#90A4AE" tabindex="0"><code><span class="line"><span style="color:#C792EA;--shiki-dark:#9C3EDA">const</span><span style="color:#EEFFFF;--shiki-dark:#90A4AE"> value </span><span style="color:#89DDFF;--shiki-dark:#39ADB5">=</span><span style="color:#EEFFFF;--shiki-dark:#90A4AE"> (window </span><span style="color:#89DDFF;font-style:italic;--shiki-dark:#39ADB5;--shiki-dark-font-style:italic">as</span><span style="color:#FFCB6B;--shiki-dark:#E2931D"> any</span><span style="color:#EEFFFF;--shiki-dark:#90A4AE">)</span><span style="color:#89DDFF;--shiki-dark:#39ADB5">.</span><span style="color:#82AAFF;--shiki-dark:#6182B8">glob</span><span style="color:#EEFFFF;--shiki-dark:#90A4AE">()</span><span style="color:#89DDFF;--shiki-dark:#39ADB5">;</span></span></code></pre>
<p>Tuttavia, questo approccio ci priva del controllo desiderato sul codice.
Fortunatamente, TypeScript offre una soluzione elegante anche per questo. È possibile estendere il tipo <code>global</code> in modo intuitivo:</p>
<pre class="shiki shiki-themes material-theme-darker material-theme-lighter" style="background-color:#212121;--shiki-dark-bg:#FAFAFA;color:#EEFFFF;--shiki-dark:#90A4AE" tabindex="0"><code><span class="line"><span style="color:#C792EA;--shiki-dark:#9C3EDA">declare</span><span style="color:#EEFFFF;--shiki-dark:#90A4AE"> global </span><span style="color:#89DDFF;--shiki-dark:#39ADB5">{</span></span>
<span class="line"><span style="color:#C792EA;--shiki-dark:#9C3EDA">    interface</span><span style="color:#FFCB6B;--shiki-dark:#E2931D"> Window</span><span style="color:#89DDFF;--shiki-dark:#39ADB5"> {</span></span>
<span class="line"><span style="color:#F07178;--shiki-dark:#E53935">        glob</span><span style="color:#89DDFF;--shiki-dark:#39ADB5">():</span><span style="color:#FFCB6B;--shiki-dark:#E2931D"> void</span><span style="color:#89DDFF;--shiki-dark:#39ADB5">;</span></span>
<span class="line"><span style="color:#89DDFF;--shiki-dark:#39ADB5">    }</span></span>
<span class="line"><span style="color:#89DDFF;--shiki-dark:#39ADB5">}</span></span>
<span class="line"><span style="color:#C792EA;--shiki-dark:#9C3EDA">const</span><span style="color:#EEFFFF;--shiki-dark:#90A4AE"> value </span><span style="color:#89DDFF;--shiki-dark:#39ADB5">=</span><span style="color:#EEFFFF;--shiki-dark:#90A4AE"> window</span><span style="color:#89DDFF;--shiki-dark:#39ADB5">.</span><span style="color:#82AAFF;--shiki-dark:#6182B8">glob</span><span style="color:#EEFFFF;--shiki-dark:#90A4AE">()</span><span style="color:#89DDFF;--shiki-dark:#39ADB5">;</span></span></code></pre>
<p>In questo modo, si ottiene il pieno controllo sul valore restituito e l’intellisense si allinea perfettamente al nuovo metodo. Un esempio ulteriore potrebbe essere l’estensione dell’interfaccia <code>Array</code>, arricchendola con il metodo <code>print()</code>:</p>
